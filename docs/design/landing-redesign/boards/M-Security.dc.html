The four proofs

What each signal proves, and how.

Moment, person, place — and a backup for place, for when the network can't answer.

Proof of moment

One-time signature

The plaque's NTAG 424 DNA chip signs every tap with a fresh counter + AES cryptogram. Copied or replayed links are rejected — you must physically be at the plaque, right now.

Under the hood

A link that works once

On every read the chip writes a fresh link: the plaque's id, a counter that only goes up, and a 16-character signature (CMAC) made with the plaque's AES-128 key.

The server checks the signature, then moves the plaque's counter forward in one database statement that refuses to go backwards. A second use of the same link finds the counter already past it and is rejected — and the rejected tap is still recorded.

Plaque keys are stored encrypted under a separate key and are never written to a log.

Proof of person

Phone session

Identity lives in a secure session on each employee's own phone, behind their own screen lock. No shared PINs, no swappable cards — the phone has to be there.

Under the hood

One phone, one person

A phone gets its session once: the employee opens their invite link, agrees, and makes a first real NFC tap at one of your plaques. That tap writes no record; the next one clocks in.

The phone keeps a random 256-bit token. The database keeps only a keyed hash of it, so the stored value cannot be used to sign in.

When you switch someone off, their next tap is rejected and the attempt is logged as a security event.

Proof of place

Site IP match

Requests from your site's WiFi carry its static IP — hard evidence the person is inside the building.

Under the hood

The network has to describe one place

The address that counts is the one the request arrives from, checked against the ranges saved for the plaque's venue. The Wi-Fi name is not evidence.

A list of ranges only counts if it is narrow enough to describe one place: at most a /7 of IPv4 addresses, or a /31 of IPv6. A wider list is refused when you save it and is not counted when a tap is judged.

Backup proof of place

GPS fallback

WiFi down or phone on mobile data? GPS confirms presence within 150 m. Anything unverified is stored, flagged, and queued for manager review — never silently approved.

Under the hood

One position, only at the tap

The tap page asks the phone for its position once, when the button is pressed. Nothing reads it in the background, before or after.

The radius is a deployment setting, 150 m by default. A position inside it counts as proof of place; a position outside it sends the tap to a manager, even if the network matched.

A QR check-in has no signature, so a position alone is never enough for it: it needs the venue's network.

Debounce is per person

A queue of people can tap the same plaque one after another, and every tap is recorded. Only the same person tapping again within 60 seconds is ignored.

The 60 seconds are timed on the server's clock, so a phone's clock cannot shorten them.

Five of the eleven guardrails are shown. The others refuse a tap page that is too old and a declared time that is implausible, keep a plaque of a business outside your group out of your records, hold a QR scan at a partner business to that venue's own network, keep rule changes to the owner and stop anyone reviewing their own record. The 60-second window and the 150 m radius are deployment settings; the figures shown are the defaults.

Corrections

Records are never edited. Corrections are new records.

The database refuses to change or delete a stored record — for the application, and for the database owner's own account too.

When a shift needs fixing, a manager adds an entry by hand. It is marked "Entered by a manager", stays apart from tapped records in reports, and the action is written to an audit log.

Privacy

No fingerprints. No tracking. No ad cookies.

No biometrics

None collected, ever

Nothing in the product reads a finger or a face. The phone's own screen lock stays the phone's.

No background location

GPS is read only at the moment of the tap. There is no geofence and no tracking between taps.

No ad or analytics cookies

Taptime sets no advertising or analytics cookie and embeds nothing from another site.

Privacy by design: Taptime stores no fingerprints, no face scans, and never tracks location — GPS is read only at the moment of the tap. Built for GDPR from day one.

Isolation

Every business is walled off in the database itself.

Every record carries the id of the business it belongs to, and the database filters every query by it. The application connects with a role that cannot switch that filter off, and its queries filter by business a second time.

When Taptime's own staff look into an account, they use a separate sign-in, and every look is written to an operator audit log.

The one sanctioned crossing is a group Taptime sets up for companies that belong together: their staff can clock in at each other's venues, and the record lands with the person's own employer.
