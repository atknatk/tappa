Late nights

A shift that ends after midnight is still one shift.

Late-night bars welcome: overnight shifts pair check-outs to the last open check-in, not the calendar date.

  • In or out is decided by the person's last open check-in, so the 02:04 tap closes the 17:58 one.
  • The hours land on the day the shift started, at the venue where it started.
  • Lateness is measured against the hours of the venue where the person tapped: Rusty Bar's 18:00, not a daytime venue's 10:00.

Every venue on its own terms

Each venue keeps its own hours and its own network.

One plaque per site, each with its own network address on file.

  • Plaques are bound to a venue, so the venue of a tap comes from the plaque, not from the phone.
  • A venue's network only counts if the range is narrow enough to describe one place; the venue page refuses a list that is too wide.

Covering another venue

Staff can cover any venue in the chain.

A tap at another venue is judged on that venue's evidence and that venue's hours, exactly as a tap at home would be.

  • The record carries a note that the tap was away from home; the employee's screen does not mention it.
  • The Roll counts those shifts as at another venue, so cover is visible without being treated as a problem.

When someone forgets

Taptime never invents a check-out.

01 A check-in nobody closed KF HAMRUN · IN 10:02 · no check-out
02 Listed for a manager Open check-ins, not counted
03 Closed by hand Entered by a manager · OUT 22:05
  • A check-in nobody closed stays open and is listed for a manager, who enters the check-out by hand.
  • Open check-ins are left out of the hours, and the Roll says how many there are.
